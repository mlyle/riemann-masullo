# Genuine theta rank and physical cutoff have two different iterated limits

Fix a number field, a theta center $a$, and widths $0\le\alpha\le\beta$ with the same degree $s\ge0$. The positive Hilbert weights are $w_\eta(x)=e^{2\eta |x|}(1+x^2)^{s/2}$. Let $I:W_\beta\to W_\alpha$ be the continuous inclusion on physical representatives, $P_\beta$ the wide spectral projection and $G_\alpha$ the narrow geometric projection. Fix one Bruhat test $\varphi$. Let $A_\eta$ be its convolution on $W_\eta$ and $C_L:W_\alpha\to W_\beta$ its mixed masked convolution at a genuine admissible radius $\Lambda=e^L$. Thus $C_L I=M_L A_\beta$. For an expanding cutoff family assume the usual $\varepsilon(\Lambda)\Lambda\to0$ and eventual positive inner band. The true multipliers $M_L$ converge strongly to the identity on the wide space, and are contractions. [Exact mixed map and signed identity](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/blob/3b6a0bac9c91cde83d14c7aa0a9201d51cecfe9e/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec4a_WidthDefect.lean#L259) · [Strong cutoff exhaustion](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/72a10ffc48c00276d45acb98b24a10816b253c54/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec4a.lean#L758)

Direct finite subsets $S$ of the physical centered polar-free theta generators by inclusion. Let $E_S$ be the orthogonal projection in $W_\alpha$ onto their finite linear span. These actual wide sources also belong to $W_\beta$, and the directed union of the finite spans is dense in $\Theta_\alpha$ by its definition. Consequently $E_S\to G_\alpha$ strongly. Define the physical finite-rank trace
$$d(L,S)=\operatorname{Tr}_{W_\beta}(C_L E_S I P_\beta).$$

**Fix the radius first.** Here $C_L$ is nuclear between the two spaces. Choose a nuclear presentation $\sum_j y_j\otimes\ell_j$ of $C_L$ with $\sum_j\|y_j\|_\beta\|\ell_j\|_{\alpha,*}<\infty$. The trace of the composite is $\sum_j \ell_j(E_S I P_\beta y_j)$; the same expression with $G_\alpha$ gives its limit. Strong convergence handles each fixed $j$, and the tails are uniformly summable since both projections and $I$ are contractions. The directed finite-span limit is therefore exactly the wide-minus-narrow cutoff trace, by the cited finite identity.

**Fix the rank first.** Give the finite span a narrow-orthonormal basis $f_1,\dots,f_n$. Each is a finite linear combination of actual theta generators, so $f_j=I g_j$ for a genuine vector $g_j\in\Theta_\beta$. Let $\ell_j$ be its narrow Hilbert coordinate. Then
$$d(L,S)=\sum_{j=1}^n \ell_j\{I P_\beta M_L A_\beta g_j\}.$$
Convolution preserves the **wide** theta space. Hence $P_\beta A_\beta g_j=0$ exactly, and strong convergence of $M_L$ makes every fixed summand tend to zero. This proof works with any coordinate functionals and any fixed finite family of wide theta inputs. [Convolution preserves the genuine theta space](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/72a10ffc48c00276d45acb98b24a10816b253c54/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec3.lean#L196)

If both quotient convolutions $T_\beta,T_\alpha$ are nuclear, the two orders therefore give
$$
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{S}d(L,S)=\operatorname{Tr}T_\beta-\operatorname{Tr}T_\alpha,\qquad
 \lim_S\lim_{L\to\infty}d(L,S)=0. \tag{1}
$$
The first equality uses the fixed-test cutoff exhaustion. The second requires no quotient nuclearity. These are the genuine projections, convolution and physical cutoffs from the project. The similar-looking full-width identity at a common center does not permit interchanging these two limits on the strength of their separate convergence. At the critical center and two positive widths, showing that they agree for the detecting tests is exactly the substantive signed comparison. [Native fixed-test exhaustion with its two explicit nuclearity assumptions](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/blob/3b6a0bac9c91cde83d14c7aa0a9201d51cecfe9e/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec4a_WidthDefect.lean#L302)

For fixed finitely many smooth radial theta inputs whose log-coordinate derivatives decay at every exponential rate, there is quantitative control even on a moving translate. Use a compactly supported smooth radial convolution profile $H_y(x)=H(x-y)$ so $A_{\beta,y}g_j(x)=(H*g_j)(x-y)$ with matching volume normalization. For a genuine cutoff of log radius $L$ and any fixed $\kappa>0$, its plateau contains $|x|\le L-1$ for all large $L$. If $L\ge(1+\kappa)y$ and $y\to+\infty$, the wide norm of $(M_L-1)A_{\beta,y}g_j$ is $O_{A,S,H,\kappa}(e^{-Ay})$ for every fixed $A>0$. Indeed $|x-y|\ge L-y-1$ on the omitted tails and $e^{\beta|x|}\le e^{\beta y}e^{\beta|x-y|}$. Every polynomial weight is absorbed by an arbitrarily small additional exponential, and the smoothed source has every required decay rate. The displayed finite sum gives the same bound for $|d(L,S;y)|$. The constants still depend on the particular finite span; control uniform enough for rank exhaustion is the missing estimate.

## An unconditional noncommuting example at a shifted center

Take the rational principal angular component, center $a=5/4$, degree $s=3$, narrow width $\alpha=1/8$ and wide width $\beta=3/2$. The narrow uncentered strip is $[9/8,11/8]$ and the wide uncentered strip is $[-1/4,11/4]$. All nontrivial rational zeros lie strictly inside the wide strip and outside the narrow strip by the Euler product and functional equation. For the other angular components a radial test acts by zero. The common-kernel theorem gives zero narrow principal quotient. [Actual strip common-kernel theorem](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/72a10ffc48c00276d45acb98b24a10816b253c54/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec2.lean#L6596)

Here the nuclearity of the wide radial quotient action follows from fixed vertical-line interpolation with the real parts specified below. We give the details to avoid assuming unconditional nuclearity on arbitrary test/width pairs. Write $z$ for the centered variable and set
$$
 D(z)=\frac{(a+z-1)\zeta(a+z)}{(3+z)^N}.
$$
Use inner contours $u=-11/8,-1/8$ (the uncentered lines $-1/8,9/8$) and outer inversion lines $v=-13/8,13/8$. The inner contours are strictly inside the weighted strip, enclose the whole positive-order completed divisor and contain no zero. On $a+z=9/8+it$ both the Euler series and its reciprocal are uniformly bounded. On $a+z=-1/8+it$ the functional equation and Stirling bound the reciprocal by a polynomial; the compact part is regular as the line misses the negative even integers. Therefore $1/D$ grows at most polynomially on both inner lines. The outer lines lie outside the wide strip. On the entire intervening band $\zeta(a+z)(a+z-1)$ has polynomial growth and no poles, so $N$ may be taken large enough that $D$ has uniform decay at least $(1+|\Im z|)^{-4}$. The denominator has no pole on this band.

For a centered compact smooth symbol $h$, its normalized principal convolution has multiplier $m(w)=\int h(x)e^{wx}\,dx$. For any input $q\in W_\beta$ write $E_w(q)=\int_G q(g)e^{w x(g)}d\mu$. Its operator norm is uniformly bounded on the inner lines by Cauchy–Schwarz, and it depends holomorphically on the open wide strip. The symbol decays faster than every power on the whole band. The interpolant
$$
 G_q(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}
 m(w)E_w(q)\frac{D(w)-D(z)}{(w-z)D(w)}dw
$$
matches $m(z)E_z(q)$ to the full zero order at each principal zero, by Cauchy's formula on the region between the inner lines. On either outer line the integral of the $1/(w-z)$ term is zero. Inverting there produces a sum of rank-one operators integrated in nuclear norm over the inner lines, with scalar density $m(w)/D(w)$ and functional $E_w$. The other vector on the half-line of sign $\pm$ is, up to the same harmless orientation and fixed Haar factors, the inverse transform of $D(v_\pm+it)/(w-v_\pm-it)$. Its magnitude is bounded uniformly in $w$ by $C e^{-13|x|/8}$ since the lines are separated and $D$ has integrable decay. It belongs to $W_\beta$ with uniform norm since $13/8>3/2$. Fourier inversion on the two lines gives the same physical radial vector by holomorphy and horizontal contour decay, and its Mellin transform is $G_q$. This is a nuclear interpolating operator. All nonprincipal jets of its radial output and of the original convolved input vanish by compact-group orthogonality. The common-kernel characterization now gives the same quotient map after convolution, proving the true wide quotient convolution is nuclear by composing with the bounded quotient and its orthogonal lift. [Detailed inversion and nuclear presentation](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/blob/4ffa1ed247556cd22edba631662ca6bc57d34dee/research/rational-nuclear-theta-convolution.md#a-nuclear-interpolating-operator)

Choose a nonzero smooth even $\psi\ge0$ supported where $6/5<e^{|x|}<4/3$. Put $H=\psi''-\psi/4$ and $h(x)=e^{(a-1/2)x}H(x)$. Use the genuine radial Bruhat test $h(x)/V$, where $V$ is the Tamagawa mass of the norm-one subgroup, so its multiplier is exactly $\mathcal Lh$. For every completed zero $\rho$, $\mathcal Lh(\rho-a)=\mathcal LH(\rho-1/2)$. The two pole evaluations in the **shifted** coordinates also vanish, and $h$ vanishes near the norm-one subgroup. The fixed-test quotient trace formula for a supplied nuclear operator counts every wide zero with full multiplicity. Its scalar is the standard rational explicit formula for $H$. All finite-place terms vanish on the support and the real term has weight $J(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-(2k+1/2)|x|}$. In the convention of (12.5.1.1) the spectral sum is minus this local term after polar removal. Consequently
$$
 \operatorname{Tr}T_\beta-\operatorname{Tr}T_\alpha
 =-\int_{\mathbb R}\psi(x)(J''(x)-J(x)/4)\,dx
 \le -6\int \psi(x)e^{-5|x|/2}\,dx<0 .
$$
Thus both legitimate limits in (1) exist and are unequal for a genuine two-positive-width example. The contributing zeros need not be located individually. This example uses the shifted center $a=5/4$. It gives a concrete obstruction to a **center-independent** interchange argument. At the critical center $a=1/2$ with $\alpha>0$, the exterior-zero problem remains open.
