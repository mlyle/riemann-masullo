# Rational zero exclusion from prime counts on any fixed power grid

Fix \(1/2\le\sigma<1\) and an increasing unbounded sequence of positive integer cutoffs \(n_j\) with
\[
 n_{j+1}-n_j\le C\,n_j^{1-\alpha}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\), where \(C\) and \(\alpha>0\) are fixed. Let \(w(t)=2-\cos t-\cos(\sqrt2t)\) and
\[
 E(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log(p)w(\log p)-\int_1^x w(\log t)\,dt .
\]
Suppose that for every \(\sigma<r<1\) there is a constant \(C_r\) with \(|E(n_j)|\le C_r n_j^r\) for all sufficiently large \(j\). Then \(\zeta(z)\ne0\) throughout \(\Re z>\sigma\); the rational principal completed divisor has nonpositive order there. The conclusion and the sampling hypothesis apply identically if one uses \(\sum_{n\le n_j}w(\log n)\) as the mean. In particular any fixed \(n_j=\lfloor j^b\rfloor\), \(1<b<\infty\), suffices. Up to \(X\) this uses \(O_b(X^{1/b})\) cutoffs. The signed upper estimate on the chosen samples is the outstanding input.

The two zero-exclusion steps below use the actual two-cosine detector. Ingham's density theorem is an unconditional estimate for ordinary zeta zeros. No pre-existing quasi-RH estimate or numerical verification is required.

## First use of the full-cutoff criterion

The difference of the two compensators at an integer \(N\) is \(O(\log(2N))\), by partitioning the integral into unit intervals and using \(|(d/dt)w(\log t)|\le(1+\sqrt2)/t\). Work with the integral convention. The weighted prime count is nondecreasing and the mean increases by at most \(4h\) over an interval of length \(h\). Thus for \(n_j\le x\le n_{j+1}\),
\[
 E(n_j)-4(x-n_j)\le E(x)\le E(n_{j+1})+4(n_{j+1}-x).
\]
Decreasing \(\alpha\) if needed, fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\). The ratio \(n_{j+1}/n_j\to1\). Put \(\theta_0=\max(\sigma,1-\alpha)<1\). The inequalities imply \(E(N)=O_r(N^r)\) for every \(r>\theta_0\) on *all* positive integers. For \(r\ge1\) use a smaller exponent \(r_0\) strictly between \(\theta_0\) and 1. Finite initial exceptions are absorbed in the constant.

For completeness, the full-cutoff criterion follows by taking the Mellin transform of \(E\). Its continuation from the original Euler half-plane is holomorphic on \(\Re s>\theta_0\) under these estimates. Multiplication by \(s\) gives the finite sum of the five translated first-prime series with their density poles removed. The higher Euler powers converge normally for \(\Re s>1/2\). Consequently the same combination of the pole-corrected negative logarithmic derivatives of zeta is holomorphic on \(\Re s>\theta_0\). At each fixed real part there, its residues give \(4m(u)=m(u+1)+m(u-1)+m(u+\sqrt2)+m(u-\sqrt2)\), where \(m\) is the nonnegative ordinary zeta zero multiplicity. Local finiteness and density of the additive group generated by \(1,\sqrt2\) supply an orbit point with zero multiplicity. Propagating zeros of \(m\) across nearest neighbors rules out any positive multiplicity on that orbit. The actual Lean join also proves the rational principal divisor conclusion. This first application shows
\(\Theta=\sup_{\rho}\Re\rho\le\theta_0<1\), where \(\rho\) ranges over nontrivial zeta zeros. [Verified full-cutoff rational criterion](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/compare/55bfd9944976e016435ac2d8e357ead5650ff77f...8c19dd0f1b0224b6c960adb2580f922620a82521) · [Arithmetic Mellin input](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/pull/7545).

## Uniform differences of actual prime counts over the gaps

Suppose \(\Theta>\sigma\). Choose \(v=(\sigma+\Theta)/2\), so \(1/2<v<\Theta\), and set \(d=3(1-v)/(2-v)<1\). Ingham's zero-density theorem gives
\[
 N(v,U)=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma:\beta\ge v,\ 0<\gamma\le U\}
 \ll_v U^d\log^5(2U).
\]
The ordinary zero count is \(O(U\log(2U))\), with multiplicities in both counts. Conjugation covers negative ordinates. [A. E. Ingham, *On the estimation of \(N(\sigma,T)\)*, Q. J. Math. 11 (1940), 201–202](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/os-11.1.201).

Take real \(X/2\le a\le b\le3X\) with \(b-a\le C'X^{1-\alpha}\), for a fixed \(C'\), and take the **same** truncation height \(U=\sqrt X/8\) at both endpoints. Theorem 1.2 of Cully-Hugill and Johnston with their parameter \(1/2\) gives uniformly for \(t\in[X/2,3X]\) and all sufficiently large \(X\),
\[
 \psi(t)=t-\sum_{|\gamma|\le U}t^\rho/\rho+O(\sqrt X\log X).
\]
Indeed \(\max(51,\log t)<U<(\sqrt t-2)/2\) throughout that range. Their \(\psi(t)\) includes all \(p^k\le t\), even at a prime-power endpoint, and the stated theorem applies to all real \(t\) in its range. Thus there is no unverified distance-to-prime assumption. Alternatively replacing both endpoints by their integer parts plus \(1/2\) leaves \(\psi\) unchanged, shifts each main term by at most \(1/2\), and puts the endpoints at least \(1/2\) away from every prime power. [Cully-Hugill and Johnston, *On the error term in the explicit formula of Riemann–von Mangoldt*, §1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10001).

For every included zero, the integral of \(t^{\rho-1}\) and the two separate endpoint terms give
\[
 \left|\frac{b^\rho-a^\rho}{\rho}\right|
 \ll_{C'} X^\beta\min\{X^{-\alpha},1/|\rho|\}.
\]
Ordinary zero counting and partial summation bound the sum of \(1/|\rho|\) through height \(U\) by \(O(\log^2(2U))\). Thus the total from \(\beta\le v\) is \(O(X^v\log^2 X)\). Put \(T=X^\alpha\); eventually \(2\le T<U\). For \(\beta>v,\ |\gamma|\le T\), use the first bound and Ingham to get \(O(X^\Theta T^{d-1}\log^5 X)\). For \(2^jT<|\gamma|\le2^{j+1}T\le 2U\), use the second to get \(O_v(X^\Theta(2^jT)^{d-1}\log^5 X)\) on each block, with the last block intersected with \([0,U]\). As \(d<1\) the geometric sum converges uniformly in the number of blocks. Hence, for these endpoints,
\[
 |\psi(b)-\psi(a)-(b-a)|
 \ll_{C',v} (\sqrt X+X^v+X^{\Theta-\alpha(1-d)})\log^5(2X).
\]
Removing all \(k\ge2\) costs \(O(\sqrt X\log^2 X)\) on the entire dyadic range: for each \(2\le k\le\log(3X)/\log2\), bound the sum over \(p\le(3X)^{1/k}\) by the corresponding sum over integers, then separate the \(k=2\) term. This establishes the same uniform bound with \(\theta\) replacing \(\psi\).

Finally put \(e_a(t)=\theta(t)-\theta(a)-(t-a)\) on \([a,b]\). Partial summation yields
\[
 E(b)-E(a)=w(\log b)e_a(b)-
    \int_a^b e_a(t)w'(\log t)\,dt/t .
\]
The uniform \(\theta\) difference estimate applies to every \(t\) in this integral. The factors \(0\le w\le4\), \(|w'|\le1+\sqrt2\) and \(\int_{X/2}^{3X}dt/t=\log6\) preserve its exponent for the exact first-prime statistic. Thus if \(\eta=\max\{v,\Theta-\alpha(1-d)\}\), then \(\eta<\Theta\) and the error changes by \(O(X^\eta\log^5(2X))\) throughout any sample gap lying at scale \(X\).

## Second use of the full-cutoff criterion

Choose \(r_1\) strictly between \(\max(\sigma,\eta)\) and \(\Theta\), and choose \(r_0\in(\sigma,r_1)\) to apply the sample hypothesis. At each integer \(N\) take the preceding cutoff \(n_j\). Both lie in \([N/2,3N]\) for all large \(N\), with \(N-n_j=O(N^{1-\alpha})\). Therefore
\[
 |E(N)|\le |E(n_j)|+|E(N)-E(n_j)|
      \ll N^{r_0}+ N^\eta\log^5(2N) \ll N^{r_1}.
\]
The same bound consequently holds at every exponent greater than \(r_1\). The full-cutoff zero-exclusion theorem at \(r_1\) forces \(\Theta\le r_1\), contradicting \(r_1<\Theta\). It follows that \(\Theta\le\sigma\). The argument makes no assumption that the supremum is attained. In \(\Re s>\sigma\), where \(\Re s>1/2\), the usual gamma factor has no zero or pole; the rational completed principal function differs from ordinary zeta there by a holomorphic nonzero factor. Its only possible positive-order points would therefore be ordinary zeta zeros; the pole at \(s=1\) is compatible with the asserted nonpositive divisor order. [Ordinary/completed rational comparison](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/compare/55bfd9944976e016435ac2d8e357ead5650ff77f...8c19dd0f1b0224b6c960adb2580f922620a82521).

For \(n_j=\lfloor j^b\rfloor\) the mean-value theorem gives \(n_{j+1}-n_j=O_b(n_j^{1-1/b})\). The general theorem therefore permits any fixed polynomial degree. An upper bound at these sample points is much stronger than the presence of arbitrarily late bounded-error crossings. The latter currently give spacings \(X^{1+o(1)}\) in the ratio-of-logarithms sense; they do not supply a bounded-error point within \(O(X^{1-\alpha})\) of every large cutoff for a fixed \(\alpha>0\).

This is a classical derivation. The full-cutoff zero detector, the quantitative integer-crossing step, and the direct perfect-square sampling specialization have Lean proofs. Ingham's density input and its arithmetic sampling application here have not been formalized.
