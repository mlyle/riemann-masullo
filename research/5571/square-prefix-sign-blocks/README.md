# Long same-sign blocks of the physical square prefix in each power interval

Put \(w(v)=2-\cos v-\cos(\sqrt2 v)\), \(E(x)=\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)w(\log p)-\int_1^x w(\log t)dt\), and \(A(k)=\sum_{j\le k}E(j^2)\). Write \(A_d\) for the version with the integer compensator. For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), every sufficiently large real \(K\) has two integer blocks inside \([K,K^{1+\varepsilon}]\), each of the form \([M,M+\lfloor M^{1-\varepsilon}\rfloor]\), with these properties.

* Throughout one block both \(A(n)/n^2\) and \(A_d(n)/n^2\) are below \(-1.25236\).
* Throughout the other both are above \(-0.74764\).

The second bound allows negative values. If RH fails and \(\Theta=\sup_\rho\Re\rho\), the same two blocks may be chosen with both signed prefix magnitudes exceeding \(n^{1+2\Theta-\varepsilon}\), negative on the first and positive on the second. This last assertion concerns individual actual prime measurements conditional only on the existence of a zero off the line. Large cutoffs may depend on \(\varepsilon\) and the zeta function. No effective initial cutoff is claimed.

## Exact absolutely summable spectrum

Set \(a=1/2\), \(B(x)=\frac12\int_1^x E(t)t^{-1/2}dt\), and \(F(y)=e^{-y}B(e^y)\). The [precise Riesz formula and the two verified degree-128 means](../signed-riesz-margin/README.md#unconditional-recurring-excursions-of-the-square-prefix) give, unconditionally for \(y\ge0\),
\[
 F(y)=D(y)+\sum_{b,\omega} C_{b,\omega}e^{(b+i\omega)y}+r(y),\qquad
 r(y)=o(1).
\]
Here \(D(y)=-1+\Re\sum_{t=1,\sqrt2} e^{ity}/[2(1+2it)(1+it)]\) is the prime-square contribution, \(b=\beta-a\) for a nontrivial zero, and \(\omega=\gamma+t\), \(t\in S=\{0,\pm1,\pm\sqrt2\}\). Group equal frequencies at each abscissa. With \(c_0=2\), \(c_{t\ne0}=-1/2\), their exact coefficient is
\[
 C_{b,\omega}= -\frac{\sum_{t\in S}c_t m(\beta+i(\omega-t))}
 {2(\beta+i\omega)(\beta+a+i\omega)}.
\]
Only nonzero coefficients are retained. One has \(\sum|C_{b,\omega}|<\infty\), \(|b|<1/2\), \(|\omega|>12\); bounded-ordinate multiplicity sums are finite. The first assertion follows from the usual zeta zero count, the last frequency bound from the first zero above 14. The coefficients have conjugate symmetry. The bounded remainder includes the kernel residues, absolutely summable trivial-zero terms, and the ordinary prime number theorem error for the squares. All higher prime powers are \(o(\sqrt x)\) before integration.

Every abscissa \(\beta\) supporting a zeta zero has at least one nonzero grouped coefficient. Otherwise its multiplicity function on that vertical line obeys \(4m(v)=m(v+1)+m(v-1)+m(v+\sqrt2)+m(v-\sqrt2)\) everywhere. For an orbit through a positive multiplicity, the dense additive subgroup generated by 1 and \(\sqrt2\) also meets a point of zero multiplicity by local finiteness. At that point nonnegativity propagates zero to all four neighbors and hence back through the orbit. [Native four-shift propagation](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/pull/7541).

## All zeros critical

In this case the series for \(F-r\) converges uniformly to a bounded uniformly almost periodic function. A positive normalized degree-128 Fejér product produces mean values \(-1\pm(128/129)V\), where \(V=\sum_{t=1,\sqrt2}1/(2|1+2it||1+it|)\). The resulting extrema pass the displayed targets strictly. For clarity, to recover blocks, choose a point where the function exceeds a target with a positive margin (or is below it), then take a small interval around it by uniform continuity. Approximate its uniformly convergent series within a fraction of this margin by a finite trigonometric polynomial. Its simultaneous approximate periods are relatively dense by compactness of the closure of its frequency orbit in a finite-dimensional torus. Indeed finitely many translates of a neighborhood of the identity cover that compact orbit closure; advance from any time by an element of the resulting bounded finite set to return to the neighborhood. Thus intervals of some fixed positive length in \(y\) with the requested inequality recur with bounded gaps.

The signed square comparison gives \(A(n)=B(n^2)+o(n^2)\) and \(A_d(n)-A(n)=O(n\log(2n))\). In the former identity the endpoint term is \(o(n^2)\) by the PNT and the signed prime rounding term is \(O(X^{5/6}\log^{11/2}(2X))\) for \(X=n^2\), from Zhai's Proposition 3.4 and partial summation. The set of \(n\) with \(2\log n\) in each fixed positive-length interval contains a consecutive integer block of length a fixed positive multiple of its starting point, once it is sufficiently large. These blocks fit even inside \([K,CK]\) for some fixed \(C\), because their logarithmic gaps are bounded. [Exact square comparison in Lean](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/pull/7592) · [Signed prime discrepancy derivation](../sparse-prime-cutoffs/README.md) · [Zhai, Proposition 3.4](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1274-27.pdf).

## Any zero off the line

Fix \(a<\theta<\Theta\), and a small desired relative tolerance in logarithmic location. Choose a nonzero spectral coefficient with \(b>\theta-a\). First choose \(d>0\) small enough for that location tolerance and \(b>(1+d)(\theta-a)\). Then choose \(\lambda>0\) small enough for all of the following requirements. At this coefficient
\[
 q_\lambda(b,\omega):=b+\lambda(b^2-\omega^2)/2>(1+d)(\theta-a).
\]
Ensure \(\lambda<1\) and \(1/2+\lambda/8-(d-\lambda/2)^2/(2\lambda)<0\), with \(d>\lambda/2\). Exclude the countable collection of \(\lambda\)'s at which the real exponents of two nonconjugate spectral terms coincide. Their affine functions of \(\lambda\) can agree identically only for equal \(b\) and equal \(|\omega|\), already a conjugate pair. The real maximum \(q_*\) exists and is separated by a positive gap from every other value: \(|b|\) is bounded, \(q_\lambda\to-\infty\) as \(|\omega|\to\infty\), and the spectrum is locally finite. It is attained by a unique conjugate pair and is positive.

Extend \(F\) to negative arguments by the displayed spectral expression with \(r=0\) there. Then \(|F(u)|\ll e^{|u|/2}\). Average against the positive unit Gaussian centered at \(y>0\) with variance \(\lambda y\). For \(z=b+i\omega\) the exact Gaussian moment is \(\exp((z+\lambda z^2/2)y)\). At fixed \(y\), absolute coefficient summability and the bound \(e^{|u|/2}\) justify termwise integration. The bounded \(D\) and \(r\) average to \(O(1)\). The resulting average equals
\[
 e^{q_*y}\,2\Re(C_* e^{i\omega_*(1+\lambda b_*)y})
       +O(e^{(q_*-c)y})+O(1)
\]
for a fixed gap \(c>0\). Since \(\omega_*\ne0\) and \(1+\lambda b_*>0\), both leading signs, with amplitudes bounded below, recur at bounded intervals in \(y\).

The integral on \(|u-y|>dy\) tends exponentially to zero. Using \(e^{|u|/2}\le e^{y/2}e^{|u-y|/2}\), completing the square and bounding each Gaussian tail gives at most a constant times \(\exp((1/2+\lambda/8-(d-\lambda/2)^2/(2\lambda))y)\). Thus each chosen sign at \(y\) is attained at some \(u\in[(1-d)y,(1+d)y]\) with \(\pm F(u)\gg e^{q_*y}>e^{(\theta-a)u}\) for large \(y\). Both Gaussian centers may be taken within a bounded distance of a prescribed point. In particular choose them near \(2(1+\varepsilon/2)\log K\) and \(d\) sufficiently small. Both resulting \(e^{u/2}\)'s are between \(K^{1+\varepsilon/4}\) and \(K^{1+3\varepsilon/4}\).

At \(n=\lfloor e^{u/2}\rfloor\), replacing \(e^u\) by \(n^2\) changes \(B\) by \(o(e^u)\), since the integration interval has length \(O(e^{u/2})\) and \(E(t)=o(t)\). The signed square comparison then gives \(\pm A(n),\pm A_d(n)\gg n^{1+2\theta}\), taking the appropriately signed location in each case. Replacing \(\theta\) by a slightly larger number first absorbs constants if needed.

Lastly the genuine prime bound \(E(x)=O_\eta(x^{\Theta+\eta})\) holds for every \(\eta>0\). For \(\Theta<1\) this follows by shifting the usual truncated Perron expression for \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) to a fixed line strictly to the right of \(\Theta\) at half-integer cutoffs, removing prime powers of total mass \(O(\sqrt x\log^2x)\), and partial summation at the two fixed frequencies. The discrete compensator adds only \(O(\log x)\). For \(\Theta=1\) the elementary Chebyshev estimate suffices. Choose \(\theta\) sufficiently close to \(\Theta\) and \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small that \(2(\Theta-\theta)+2\eta<\varepsilon\). Moving at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}\) integer steps changes either square prefix by \(O(n^{1-\varepsilon+2\Theta+2\eta})=o(n^{1+2\theta})\). The entire consecutive block therefore retains its sign with magnitude exceeding \(j^{1+2\Theta-\varepsilon}\) at each new index \(j\). The reserved space in the enclosing power interval absorbs its length. [Classical prime bounds and original inputs](../upper-riesz-real-zeros/README.md#higher-degree-finite-order-and-infinite-order-families).

The argument proves neither eventual negativity nor a positive value unconditionally. It gives same-sign blocks in every sufficiently large power interval and distinguishes the quantitative consequences of a zero off the critical line.
